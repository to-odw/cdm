Our work

Cambridge Digital Minds studies whether AI systems could one day have minds with feelings and a capacity for welfare that warrants moral consideration. Experts are divided, and a growing number of people will come to believe they do. Whatever the truth, these perceptions will have profound implications for society and policy.

Research here is interdisciplinary and rigorous, aimed at anticipating societal responses to potential digital minds and at developing governance strategies. Capacity building strengthens the field through fellowships, training programs, and an online course anchored in academic rigour. Engagement builds expert coordination with policymakers, industry, and the public, to reduce confusion and polarisation before high-stakes conflicts emerge.

AI Consciousness Will Divide Society

Experts and the public are already split on whether AI could be conscious, and on whether a digital mind would deserve moral consideration. The chapter argues that split will not close. It will harden into unstable regulation and political disagreement.

Book chapter. Caviola, L. 17 June 2026.

Human-AI Coexistence

Social AI already teaches, comforts, and coordinates with people. The paper sets out three stages of living with it: everyday roles now, institutional roles next, and, later, systems that may have minds of their own.

Paper. Caviola, L., Keeling, G., Street, W. and Shevlin, H. 15 June 2026.

Denying Personhood to AI

Since 2022, twelve U.S. states have seen bills that deny AI legal personhood, most of them copied from the same templates. The paper treats that rush as premature: it closes policy options before anyone knows what the systems can do.

Paper. Smith, A., Caviola, L. and Alexander, H. 4 June 2026.

Strategy workshop

8–9 August 2026Cambridge University

A two-day, output-oriented workshop bringing together fellows, mentors, and invited experts for structured, collaborative research on neglected strategic questions about how society should prepare for the emergence of digital minds.

We have a very limited number of spaces available for this workshop and intend to bring together a small cross-disciplinary group drawn from digital minds, AI policy and governance, and macrostrategy.

Session topics

  • Mapping the societal intervention landscape
  • Systematic comparison of policy and institutional approaches
  • Scenario planning for plausible future trajectories
  • Macrostrategy development
  • Research priorities for strategy and governance

Workshop output

Unlike typical academic conferences, these sessions are designed to contribute towards a comprehensive report that lays the groundwork for what policy and governance frameworks should look like in this emerging field.

Fellowship connection

All fellows from the Digital Minds Fellowship are invited to attend the workshop, allowing them to contribute to research outputs and build connections with senior experts in the field.
