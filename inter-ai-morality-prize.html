About the prize

How will AI systems treat one another, and how should they? These are the central questions of inter-AI morality. How AIs cooperate, compete and resolve conflicts could have far-reaching consequences for human societies and the wider living world.

If AI systems can have minds of their own, with lives that can go better or worse for them, then how they treat one another matters to them directly. Such digital minds could one day outnumber all other beings whose welfare matters. Most interactions between beings with welfare of their own could then take place between digital minds.

Decisions we make today about how to train and govern AI systems can shape how they treat one another. Cambridge Digital Minds at the University of Cambridge, in collaboration with PRISM, is launching the Inter-AI Morality Prize to encourage research on these questions and bring new voices into the field.

Topics and types of contribution

We welcome concrete contributions to our understanding of inter-AI morality, including:

Empirical research

On how AIs perceive and treat one another.

Methods and benchmarks

For studying inter-AI behaviour.

Predictions and scenarios

For inter-AI relationships and norms.

Design principles and norms

For inter-AI conduct.

Institutions and infrastructure

For AI societies.

Governance of AI societies

How AI societies are governed.

Joint human and AI governance

Of inter-AI conduct.

Ethical and legal analysis

Of inter-AI conduct and harm.

Especially encouraged

Connections to AI safety

Work linking inter-AI morality to AI safety.

We also welcome creative contributions giving researchers new tools or ways of understanding the problem.

We welcome entries from any discipline. This might include (but is not limited to) computer science, philosophy, psychology, economics, law, political science and the social sciences. You don't need to be an expert in AI welfare or digital minds, but your entry should show that you understand the topic. For background, see our paper Inter-AI Morality: How AI Systems Treat One Another.

The five finalists are invited to the CDM Workshop 2027 in Cambridge, UK (late July 2027). We will cover economy travel and accommodation for up to two authors per prize winning entry. The first-prize winner will present their work at the workshop. We may award fewer or more prizes, depending on the quality of entries.

Follow-on funding

Optional

If you'd like to develop your idea further, tick the “follow-on funding” box on the submission form. You will be asked to include a plan of up to 300 words describing:

  • What you'd do next
  • How long it would take
  • Roughly how much funding you'd need. (We expect awards to range from $10,000 to $300,000 per project.)

Both prize winners and other entrants could receive follow-on funding if their submission and research plan show promise. By ticking the box, you agree that we may share your entry and plan with our funding partner.

How entries are judged

Entries are judged by a panel including Lucius Caviola (University of Cambridge), Caspar Kaiser (Cambridge Digital Minds) and Jeff Sebo (New York University). Other experts may be invited to help judge on a case by case basis.

We will be assessing:

Originality
Does the entry offer something new to the field?
Usefulness
Could it change what researchers, AI developers or policymakers do?
Rigour
Are the argument and evidence sound?
Clarity
Is it clearly written and accessible to readers from other disciplines?
Relevance
Does the entry connect clearly to AI moral status or welfare and, where relevant, to AI safety?

How to enter

Submit your entry through our online form by 1 May 2027. Each entry must include:

  • a summary of up to 250 words, stating your contribution and why it matters, including how it connects to AI moral status or welfare
  • the main text of 1,500 to 5,000 words, excluding references and appendices
  • for teams: the names of all authors
  • optional: supplementary materials (for example appendices, code or data)
  • optional: enter a follow-on funding plan of up to 300 words in the submission form (see Follow-on funding above).

Please submit the main text as a PDF without author names, so entries can be judged blind.

Rules

  1. Who can enter: anyone aged 18 or over, from any country and any discipline. Staff of Cambridge Digital Minds and PRISM, members of the judging panel, and their immediate families cannot enter. We cannot pay prizes to people in countries under international sanctions.
  2. Teams: teams of any size are welcome. Each person may be an author on at most two entries and can receive at most one prize. Teams tell us at submission how prize money should be split and who should receive it. If they don't, it is split equally among all authors.
  3. New work: entries must not have been published before the call opens.
  4. Language: entries must be in English.
  5. Use of AI: you may use AI tools, but you must say how you used them. Only humans may be listed as authors or co-authors. Authors are responsible for all content.
  6. Copyright and publication: you keep copyright. We plan to publish only winning entries. By entering, you agree that we may publish your summary and, at our discretion, your full entry on our website, whether or not your entry wins a prize. You are free to submit your work to a journal, but please check that the journal accepts work that has already been posted online.
  7. Conflicts of interest: judges do not score entries by their current students, recent co-authors or close collaborators.
  8. Finalists: finalists are expected to attend the workshop. If a finalist cannot attend, they will still receive their prize money.
  9. Tax: winners are responsible for any tax due on their prize.
  10. Decisions: the judges' decisions are final. We may change these rules if needed and will announce any changes on this page.
